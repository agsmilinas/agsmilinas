§ 00 — About

Short bio

I'm a Ph.D. candidate in Computer Science at McGill University, affiliated with Mila (Quebec AI Institute) and The Neuro. I study how to design, scale, and rigorously evaluate intelligent computational systems, using high-resolution multimodal data and simulation as demanding scientific use cases. My methodological interests include scientific machine learning, GPU computing, computer vision, multimodal modeling, AI agents, and trustworthy AI.

My path began with a B.Sc. in Mechatronics Engineering from ITESM (Mexico) and a Master's in Computer Science and Engineering from UNAM, where I developed a real-time sign-language translator using deep convolutional networks. My doctoral work combines scalable data-processing frameworks, region-specific dynamic models, and LLM-based orchestration for reproducible simulation.

I've taught and supported courses at McGill and the Université de Montréal, served as a guest professor in Big Data & Analytics at Universidad Iberoamericana, and lectured on Bioinformatic Data Analysis at TH Bingen in summer 2026.

Alongside academia, I bring over a decade of industry experience: Lead Data Scientist at Distillery, AI/ML Cloud Solutions Engineer at Google, and Senior Data Scientist at Coca-Cola, with earlier roles at IBM, Lumiata, and P&G. Across these roles, my work has focused on modeling and deploying scalable AI systems and leading cross-functional teams across machine learning, big data, and cloud infrastructure.

AI methods, scientific computing & validation

My work develops scalable algorithms, trustworthy agentic workflows, and scientific models for data-intensive problems. Computational neuroscience provides a rigorous testbed for assessing whether these systems are fast, reproducible, and scientifically faithful.

Scalability · Scientific ML

ScaleNet

A doctoral research framework for processing high-resolution multimodal data and building multiscale, region-specific dynamic models. Seg-Scale addresses scalable imaging workflows; DynNet investigates cross-scale coupling and simulations.

Workshop paper
Bifurcation Preservation — AI4Physics @ ICML 2026

Accepted at the AI4Physics Workshop at ICML 2026 (Seoul, July 2026): “Bifurcation Preservation as a Physics Diagnostic for Neural Phase-Field Surrogates.”

The study shows that low rollout error does not necessarily imply faithful physical behavior: a surrogate can reconstruct a trajectory well while shifting the system's dissolve/persist bifurcation boundary. Official proceedings / PDF.
